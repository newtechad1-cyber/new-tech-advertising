# NTA AI Publishing Operating System

Status: Proposed operating-system blueprint  
Owner: Rick Hesse / New Tech Advertising  
Authoritative implementation source: GitHub  
Long-term knowledge repository: Google Drive  
Deployment environment: Base44  
Last updated: 2026-07-29

## Purpose

The NTA Publishing Platform should not become another collection of disconnected tools. It should turn one useful conversation, one lived experience, or one business lesson into a connected set of durable assets.

The operating principle is:

> Start with what Rick knows. Preserve his voice. Approve the idea once. Then adapt it into the forms each channel needs.

The core workflow is:

**Conversation → Article → Script → HeyGen Video → YouTube → Website Lesson → Social Media → Knowledge Library**

This is not eight separate content projects. It is one approved knowledge asset moving through eight controlled stages.

## Source-of-truth rules

1. ChatGPT and spoken conversation are the thinking and development space.
2. Google Drive preserves Rick's raw source material, approved manuscripts, long-term knowledge, and human-readable masters.
3. GitHub is authoritative for publishing schemas, website content, scripts used by the platform, workflow definitions, and implementation code.
4. HeyGen renders an approved video script; it does not become the source of the lesson.
5. Descript is the optional correction and finishing room, not a required stop for every video.
6. YouTube is the video library and discovery channel.
7. The NTA website is the canonical public lesson and conversion path.
8. Social platforms distribute excerpts that lead people back to the full lesson.
9. Base44 deploys what GitHub defines. Avoid manual Base44 changes when the same work can be reviewed and preserved in GitHub.

## Voice preservation contract

Every asset must remain grounded in Rick's lived experience.

- Write in first person when the idea comes from Rick's own story.
- Prefer plainspoken explanation over technical terminology.
- Preserve uncertainty where Rick expresses uncertainty.
- Do not invent events, numbers, clients, conversations, motives, or timelines.
- Do not turn one personal experience into a universal psychological claim.
- Do not make Rick sound like a generic AI promoter.
- Keep the business principle visible, but earn it through the story.
- When adapting an article into a spoken script, shorten sentences without replacing Rick's meaning.
- Keep a small set of verbatim anchor lines approved by Rick. These create continuity across the article, video, website, and social posts.
- If a draft sounds polished but could have been written by anyone, it is not ready.

## New lesson development map

### 1. I Used to Sell Air. Now I Sell Proof.

Status: Ready to Develop  
Primary home: NTA Knowledge Library  
Supporting formats: article, video lesson, talk, LinkedIn/Facebook story

Core idea: Rick once sold advertising largely through reach, audience estimates, schedules, reputation, and promises about what exposure might produce. Modern marketing creates the opportunity to show evidence through analytics, tracking, dashboards, customer journeys, conversion data, and follow-up. The lesson is not that old advertising had no value. The contrast is that Rick can now help an owner see what happened after the message went out.

Lived-experience anchors:

- Rick sold television advertising.
- Traditional media often required an owner to trust the seller and the medium.
- Rick's present direction is to connect promotion to observable business results.
- Proof should include the entire customer path, not merely clicks and impressions.

Important distinction: attribution is imperfect. Do not replace the old promise with a new false certainty. The lesson should explain levels of evidence: exposure, engagement, inquiry, qualified opportunity, sale, repeat business, and referral.

Recommended hook: "I spent years selling something a business owner could not hold in his hand. Today, I want to show him what happened."

### 2. The Hidden Economics of AI

Status: Ready to Develop  
Primary home: Practical AI / Business Foundations crossover  
Supporting formats: article, short explainer series, cost worksheet

Core idea: AI offers are often marketed as if software has nearly unlimited margins and every feature is almost free. In reality, AI systems may incur model, token, image, video, storage, retrieval, integration, support, and infrastructure costs every time they are used. Business owners need enough understanding to compare value, not enough technical detail to become engineers.

Required distinctions:

- subscription price versus usage-based cost;
- included allowance versus overage;
- user seat versus API consumption;
- inexpensive text operation versus expensive video or media generation;
- software gross margin versus the customer's business value;
- fixed monthly expense versus variable cost per completed job;
- price of a tool versus cost of the whole workflow;
- cost savings versus revenue or service improvement.

Evaluation question: "What useful business result does this cost produce?"

Do not present current vendor prices as timeless. Store dated research notes and link to official pricing sources.

### 3. Technology Always Moves Faster Than Business

Status: Ready to Develop  
Primary home: Business change / AI Foundations bridge  
Supporting formats: historical video, keynote-style lesson, timeline social series

Core idea: businesses have always needed time to understand and absorb new technology. Television, computers, the Internet, smartphones, social media, and AI did not arrive as finished business systems. The technology appeared first. Useful practices, standards, skills, trust, and workflows developed afterward.

Rick's advantage is not predicting every technology. It is having lived through several shifts in how businesses communicate and operate.

Avoid a shallow technology timeline. The lesson should examine the repeating adoption pattern:

1. novelty and exaggerated promises;
2. confusion and resistance;
3. scattered experimentation;
4. practical use cases;
5. connected business processes;
6. ordinary infrastructure.

The lesson should reassure owners without encouraging delay: they do not need to chase everything, but they do need a method for learning and adapting.

### 4. Connecting the Tools

Status: Ready to Develop  
Primary home: Practical AI / personal business experience  
Supporting formats: article, video, workflow demonstration

Core idea: Rick bought or tested hundreds of AI tools while searching for practical value. The turning point was realizing that individual capabilities matter less than how information and work move from one step to the next.

This is the personal journey lesson. Preserve the frustration, cost, hope, experimentation, and gradual recognition of the larger system. Do not turn it into a generic list of recommended apps.

Ending direction: a tool earns its place only when its input, output, owner, next step, cost, and source of truth are understood.

### 5. Tools Don't Build Businesses. Systems Do.

Status: Ready to Develop  
Primary home: NTA operating philosophy / cornerstone lesson  
Supporting formats: article, flagship video, sales explanation, system-assessment worksheet

Core idea: a person can own every capable tool available and still have no dependable business process. Experience and wisdom are required to connect tools into workflows that match how people think, how customers decide, and how the business actually performs work.

Relationship to "Connecting the Tools":

- "Connecting the Tools" is Rick's autobiographical journey.
- "Tools Don't Build Businesses. Systems Do." is the transferable business principle learned from that journey.

Keep both lessons. Avoid repetition by assigning story to the first and framework to the second.

System design questions:

- What problem is the workflow solving?
- Who begins it?
- What information must be known?
- Where is that information preserved?
- What decision is AI allowed to support?
- Where must a human approve or take responsibility?
- What output is produced?
- What happens next?
- How do we know whether it worked?
- What does it cost each time it runs?

### 6. The Insecurity That Drove Me

Status: Keep Exploring; autobiographical source collection required before drafting  
Primary homes: NTA and Talking About Jesus  
Supporting formats: two related but distinct essays

Core idea: insecurity motivated much of Rick's life, while ego sometimes became a mask that protected or hid that insecurity. This must be written as Rick's interpretation of his own life, not a diagnosis of other people and not a universal rule.

Shared source, two editorial treatments:

**NTA version:** how insecurity, proving himself, selling, achievement, failure, leadership, and business identity interacted; what Rick understands differently now.

**Talking About Jesus version:** how insecurity, ego, failure, humility, grace, identity, and Rick's changing understanding of Jesus interacted.

Boundaries:

- Gather specific memories before building the narrative.
- Use phrases such as "I can see now," "in my life," and "I believe" where appropriate.
- Do not retrofit motives onto events Rick has not discussed.
- Do not use psychological labels beyond Rick's own language.
- Do not make the faith version preach at the reader.
- The NTA and faith versions may share a source note, but each needs its own purpose, ending, and related links.

## Complete publishing workflow

### Stage 1 — Conversation

Inputs:

- Rick's spoken or typed conversation;
- relevant earlier lessons and memories;
- client or business examples Rick has approved;
- factual research where the subject requires it.

Outputs:

- source transcript or notes in Drive;
- concise idea record in the Working Brain;
- content record ID and working title;
- voice anchors: phrases or passages that sound unmistakably like Rick;
- unresolved questions and factual claims requiring verification.

Human gate: Rick confirms that the central idea has been understood.

ChatGPT's role: listen, organize, identify connections, ask only questions that materially improve accuracy, and distinguish Rick's experience from outside research.

Descript's role: optional transcription and cleanup for long recordings. Do not require Descript when ChatGPT voice transcription is already sufficient.

### Stage 2 — Article

The article is the fullest approved expression of the idea.

Required fields:

- title;
- slug;
- collection and audience;
- summary and primary takeaway;
- full article body;
- lived-experience anchors;
- claims and sources;
- search description and primary search phrase;
- related NTA lessons and internal links;
- reflection questions;
- status;
- version and approval date.

Human gate: Rick approves meaning and voice before downstream generation.

GitHub stores the publishable structured content. Drive stores the human-readable approved master.

### Stage 3 — Script

Create the script from the approved article, never from an early outline.

Default formats:

- full YouTube lesson: approximately 4–8 minutes;
- discovery video or Short: 30–60 seconds;
- social excerpt: one idea and one invitation, not a compressed version of the whole lesson.

Script adaptation rules:

- write for the ear;
- keep short sentences and natural contractions;
- preserve the hook and key takeaway;
- use one central idea per video;
- identify exact on-screen phrases separately;
- include pronunciation notes when needed;
- specify visuals only where they improve understanding;
- link every script to its source article ID and version.

Human gate: Rick approves the script before paid generation.

### Stage 4 — HeyGen Video

Use HeyGen as the renderer, not the writer of record.

Default NTA production profile:

- 16:9 landscape for the full YouTube and website lesson;
- presenter-led, warm, practical, educational tone;
- selected Rick avatar and approved voice when available;
- NTA brand colors and restrained motion graphics;
- on-screen text for the key sentence, not every spoken word;
- stock footage only when it clarifies a real setting;
- minimal futuristic imagery;
- one approved script per generation.

Cost-control rules:

1. Generate only from an approved script.
2. Use the current Creator subscription before considering a separate API plan.
3. Prefer a simple presenter-led render for routine lessons.
4. Use full Video Agent treatment selectively for flagship lessons where automatically produced B-roll and motion graphics justify the higher credit rate.
5. Test the first 30–60 seconds before spending credits on a long flagship video when style or voice is uncertain.
6. Do not generate both landscape and portrait versions from scratch by default. Produce the landscape master, then cut approved excerpts in Descript.
7. Record the credit estimate before generation and the actual usage afterward.
8. Revisions should identify the exact defect; do not regenerate unchanged prompts.
9. Keep source script, HeyGen session link, video ID, render date, duration, orientation, avatar, voice, and cost in the content record.
10. Stop automatic generation when the monthly budget threshold is reached.

### Stage 5 — Descript finishing

Descript is optional and should be used when a video needs:

- transcript-based corrections;
- removal of pauses or failed passages;
- NTA intro/outro;
- captions and styling;
- audio leveling;
- landscape-to-portrait excerpts;
- reusable clips;
- final quality review.

Avoid routing every clean HeyGen video through a complicated editing project. A tool should remain optional when it does not improve the result.

Outputs:

- final master MP4;
- caption file if available;
- portrait clips;
- final transcript;
- thumbnail frame or notes for thumbnail production.

### Stage 6 — YouTube

YouTube is the permanent public video shelf and discovery channel.

Required package:

- title aligned with the website lesson;
- plainspoken description;
- canonical website lesson URL near the top;
- chapters for longer videos;
- related NTA lessons;
- thumbnail;
- playlist/collection assignment;
- subtitles;
- pinned comment leading to the website lesson;
- YouTube video ID and publish date returned to the content record.

Do not publish automatically in Phase 1. Prepare the complete upload package and require Rick's approval.

### Stage 7 — Website lesson

The website lesson is the canonical public learning page.

The page should include:

- complete article;
- embedded YouTube video;
- transcript or accessible summary;
- related lessons;
- clear next lesson;
- invitation to the NTA Growth Guide or appropriate conversation;
- structured metadata;
- updated date;
- canonical URL.

GitHub receives the video ID and publication metadata in a small content update. This is preferable to manually wiring the video in Base44.

### Stage 8 — Social distribution

Social content should come from the approved lesson record.

Default package:

- Facebook post in Rick's conversational voice;
- LinkedIn post emphasizing the business principle;
- one 30–60 second vertical excerpt;
- one quote or idea card;
- YouTube Short when the excerpt stands alone;
- all links directed to the canonical lesson or full YouTube video according to the campaign goal.

Social media is discovery, not the archive. Do not create a new argument on social that is missing from the approved lesson.

### Stage 9 — Knowledge Library preservation

After publication, update Drive and the GitHub content record with:

- final article;
- final script;
- final transcript;
- canonical URL;
- YouTube URL;
- social package;
- source research;
- publication date;
- revision history;
- related lessons;
- outcomes or learning notes.

The lesson is then marked Published Master. Future adaptations begin from this approved version.

## Automation boundary

Phase 1 should be assisted and approval-based:

- automatically organize and prepare;
- manually approve the article;
- manually approve the script;
- manually approve paid video generation;
- manually approve public publishing.

Do not begin with unattended end-to-end automation. The first goal is a dependable editorial system. After 3–5 lessons move through it successfully, automate only the repeated steps that have remained stable.

Good later automation candidates:

- generating derivative social copy from an approved lesson;
- creating a YouTube metadata package;
- checking that required fields are complete;
- adding a returned YouTube ID to the website lesson;
- updating publication status;
- preserving final transcripts and links;
- producing cost and performance summaries.

Keep human approval for voice, claims, paid generation, and public release.

## Implementation phases

### Phase 1 — Blueprint and pilot

- Preserve the six lesson ideas.
- Add the content-record schema and status model.
- Choose one pilot lesson: "Connecting the Tools."
- Develop and approve the article.
- Create one 4–6 minute landscape script.
- Create one low-cost presenter-led HeyGen test.
- Finish only if needed in Descript.
- Prepare—but do not automatically publish—the YouTube, website, and social packages.
- Record actual time, credits, corrections, and friction.

### Phase 2 — Platform support

- Add an internal publishing queue to the existing back office.
- Show source status, approval gates, links, costs, and missing fields.
- Add script and video metadata panels.
- Add "prepare package" actions that produce drafts without public writes.
- Connect existing subscriber/contact and publication systems; do not create a duplicate CRM.

### Phase 3 — Controlled publishing

- Connect YouTube publishing after destination and permissions are verified.
- Add website lesson update from the authoritative GitHub content record.
- Add optional Descript project creation for videos that need editing.
- Add social scheduling only after channel-specific review.

### Phase 4 — Learning loop

- bring YouTube retention, clicks, website engagement, inquiries, and qualitative feedback back to the lesson record;
- distinguish reach metrics from business outcomes;
- use evidence to improve future hooks, length, visuals, and calls to action;
- keep the article and core idea stable unless new learning justifies a revision.

## Pilot definition of done

"Connecting the Tools" has completed the pilot when:

- the source conversation is preserved;
- Rick has approved the full article;
- the article is stored in Drive and GitHub;
- Rick has approved a spoken script;
- the HeyGen render and actual credit use are recorded;
- any Descript edits are complete;
- a final MP4 and transcript exist;
- the YouTube package is complete;
- the website lesson is ready with an embed location;
- Facebook, LinkedIn, and one vertical clip are prepared;
- the canonical lesson record links every asset;
- no Base44 credits were used for work that GitHub could preserve and deploy.

## Governing principle

NTA is not building a content machine that manufactures more material.

It is building a knowledge system that takes what Rick has learned through advertising, sales, technology, business, failure, faith, and conversation—and preserves it in forms that can keep teaching people.

The tools are replaceable. The connected knowledge, Rick's voice, and the operating system are the durable assets.
